# The First-Gas Question — Rayls Enygma Public

**Research checkpoint, 21 November 2026. Not the final Quest submission.**

## The experiment
Can a newcomer register, receive a shielded invitation and spend without ever publicly funding their EVM account? We compare ordinary public funding, an invitation claim, and direct shielded faucet onboarding; independently measure public contract logs, receipts, account balances and fee routing; and keep private wallet observations separate from public-chain proof.

### Private fee metadata — exploratory side finding

Although the two private vault transfers reveal no private payment principal publicly, subsequent **separate public RLS transfers** from the relayer to the treasury address reported by the relayer config matched the **15/115** gross-fee split exactly.

- Invitation: vault block **24,744,028**, payout `0x3650f335ce12fe0c2ad4616aa7d595503e44e18b141e35121fc7cc7f077377f1` in block 24,744,032, 0.576 RLS to treasury, implying **4.416 RLS** gross fee, matching the wallet's rounded 4.42.
- Claim: vault block **24,747,924**, payout `0xc9e3b527d27b152bde59a9529679dcbf2d4237dda1c7f7c2564ad9cff18ab116` in block 24,747,926, **1.656 RLS**, implying **12.696 RLS** gross fee. This matches the wallet's 12.70 quote and exact 30−12.696=17.304 wallet result.
- In separate **−12/+25-block** windows, each operation was the only vault-nullifier transaction and had exactly one treasury transfer from the reported relayer. A post-registration, zero-public-fee control produced **zero matching payout transfers** in a separate +35-block window.
- This *does not establish a cryptographic source→payout mapping*. Correlation is temporally and arithmetically supported in **two** controlled samples but not network-wide. Treasury identity is relayer-reported. It is **fee metadata**, not inferred private principal/recipient.

### Historical-state replay controls

The original invite and claim calldata were re-executed **read-only** via `eth_call` as the public relayer, against the state immediately before mining, at the mined block and one block afterward. Both calls returned ABI `true` **before** mining, and both reverted at/after the mined block. These are **2 positive and 4 negative** noncommitting simulation results. They support state-dependent replay rejection consistent with spent nullifiers, **not** a definitive diagnosis of the revert cause or a security proof against every replay scenario.

Source `qa/verify_private_replay_controls.py`; evidence `evidence/public-rpc/controlled/claim-invite-historical-replay-controls.json`.
